Where to elope in Charleston.
A setting for your story.

Compare Charleston elopement location ideas through the details that make a day work: guest count, privacy, access, weather, and what happens after the ceremony.

Local information checked October 11, 2026. These are location ideas, not confirmed partnerships or reservations.

The best Charleston elopement location is the one that suits your people and your plans. A garden can offer a lush backdrop, a downtown courtyard can keep the day close together, and a beach can create a wonderfully open atmosphere. Each also comes with practical questions that a photograph cannot answer.

ADG Hospitality’s experiences are intended for couples and up to 30 guests, subject to location rules. That ceiling does not mean every setting can accommodate your full group. Begin with your guest count and priorities, then request availability and a written scope before committing to a place.

White Point Garden: a downtown outdoor setting

If you are drawn to a ceremony beneath trees near the Battery, White Point Garden is one location to explore. Think through how guests will arrive, where they can sit if permitted, and the route to your photographs or dinner afterward. Treat the park as a shared public setting rather than assuming you will have exclusive use.

The City of Charleston limits events here to 25 people total, including the couple. It requires a park permit and asks you to contact Recreation at least 21 business days before the event. Confirm how your officiant, photographer, and other professionals count toward the limit. See the city’s park event requirements.

Waterfront Park: consider the complete experience

A waterfront setting may appeal if harbor views are part of your Charleston vision. Evaluate the proposed ceremony spot in person or through a detailed planning conversation. Ask about foot traffic, sound, shade, permitted setup, accessibility, and whether the atmosphere at your ceremony hour suits the quiet moment you imagine.

Waterfront Park has the same city event limit of 25 people total, including the couple. A permit is subject to approval; the city says dates are secured once the required fees are paid and an approved permit is issued. Review official availability and permit guidance.

A private courtyard or boutique property

For couples who want their ceremony, photographs, and dinner close together, a private courtyard or a small hospitality property is worth discussing. The appeal is the possibility of a connected day, with fewer transitions for you and your guests. Actual privacy, access, and event permissions vary by property.

Ask whether the venue permits weddings, whether guest rooms must be reserved, and what event fees or minimum spending requirements apply. Confirm ceremony and meal capacities separately. A room advertised for 30 people may hold fewer once a ceremony aisle or dining tables are introduced. An ordinary accommodation booking is not permission to host an event.

Gardens and estate settings

A reserved garden can be a good direction when you want greenery and a more defined ceremony space. Compare the allowed event window, photography access, setup restrictions, restroom access, and weather alternatives. Ask whether other visitors will be nearby and whether the listed guest count includes your professionals.

One published example is Magnolia Plantation & Gardens, whose elopement offering lists the couple plus up to 30 guests, with Monday–Friday ceremonies between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. in a two-hour window. Those are the venue’s terms, not an ADG package inclusion; confirm current availability and pricing directly. Read Magnolia’s official elopement information.

Some estate venues in this region are former plantations with histories of enslavement. Consider that history, the property’s interpretation of it, and your guests’ comfort as part of your decision. A setting should align with your values as well as your visual preferences.

Folly Beach: plan the ceremony and celebration separately

If sand and ocean are central to your vision, consider the ceremony footprint, tide, wind, guest mobility, and a backup before selecting a beach access point. Keep the setup suited to the site and plan your meal at an appropriate location afterward.

Folly Beach states that the public beach cannot be reserved and beach weddings with more than 25 people require Special Events Committee approval. Wedding receptions and alcohol are prohibited on the beach, and vendors need city business licenses. These details are especially relevant to a larger elopement group. Check Folly Beach’s official wedding rules.

Isle of Palms: another coastal direction

Isle of Palms is another setting to explore if you prefer a coastal ceremony. Choose the exact location based on access and the experience of your whole group. Plan for parking, a route across the sand, and the possibility of other beachgoers near the ceremony.

The city’s wedding guidance states that a paid beach photographer needs a city business license and other businesses, such as catering, are prohibited on the beach. It also advises checking the tide and city calendar. Review the full rules for your specific plans rather than assuming all beach locations work alike. Read Isle of Palms’ official beach wedding guidance.

Choose a shortlist before choosing a date

Pick two location styles you love, then compare them on the same questions: Can everyone attend comfortably? Is the ceremony permitted? What privacy do you have? Is there a suitable backup? What are the fees and access hours? How far is the next part of the day?

A brief with those answers is far more useful than a long list of pretty places. Share your date range, guest count, and priorities with ADG Hospitality.
