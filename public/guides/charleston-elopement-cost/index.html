What does a Charleston
elopement cost?

Start with a clear investment, then build a celebration around what matters to you. A smaller guest list can create room for a more considered experience.

Package starting investments supplied by ADG Hospitality. Local fee information checked October 11, 2026.

The cost of an elopement in Charleston depends on more than how many people attend. Your ceremony location, date, photography coverage, flowers, seating, and plans afterward all shape the total. A ceremony for two and a hosted celebration for 30 guests can have very different needs, even when both feel intimate.

For ADG Hospitality, the starting investments are straightforward: Petite $3,500, Signature $5,000, and Premier $7,500. These are starting prices for the package experience, not a statement that every possible wedding expense is included. Your final location and scope will be confirmed in a written proposal before you book.

What changes the final investment?

The setting and access

A permitted public location, a reserved garden, and a private courtyard do not offer the same access or privacy. Ask about venue fees, preparation space, event duration, setup time, and an indoor backup. A beautiful location may need fewer decorative elements, while a private space may offer more control over the experience.

The people you invite

Guest count affects seating, transport, refreshments, and any meal. When you move from a ceremony for two to a gathering of family and friends, budget for the practical work of welcoming everyone. Giving each guest a comfortable place to sit can matter more than adding another decorative feature.

Photography and design

Confirm how much photography coverage you need and what the final delivery includes. Getting-ready photographs, the ceremony, group portraits, couple portraits, and dinner coverage require different amounts of time. For flowers and styling, agree on the actual pieces and scale rather than relying on an inspiration image alone.

Build one budget for the whole trip

Separate the package investment from the rest of your plans. Depending on the final proposal, costs beyond your package may include travel, accommodation, attire, rings, personal styling, venue or permit charges, a marriage license, transport, restaurant reservations, taxes, service charges, and optional gratuities. Some services may be included; the written proposal should make that clear.

For example, selecting Signature’s $5,000 starting investment does not automatically set the budget for a three-night stay and a dinner for 20. List those decisions separately, then decide what total feels comfortable before adding enhancements. This is a planning exercise, not an estimate of third-party prices.

A small, specific legal expense

Charleston County lists its marriage-license application fee as $70 plus a transaction fee, with a mandatory 24-hour wait and an emailed license within two business days after a completed application. This is a separate government process. Check the county’s current fee and instructions.

Compare the scope, not just the headline price

When comparing elopement packages, ask what “planning” actually covers. Does someone source and book the professionals, create the timeline, communicate with them, and guide the day? Are you paying for a coordinated experience, or receiving a list of recommendations to manage yourself? Both can be useful, but they should be described clearly.

A helpful proposal answers these questions:

  • Which location and services are included, and what remains your responsibility?
  • What are the ceremony window, photography hours, and guest allowance?
  • Are permits, venue charges, seating, flowers, and taxes accounted for?
  • What is the weather backup, and does it add cost?
  • What are the payment schedule, cancellation terms, and rescheduling options?

If a detail matters to you, have it written into the scope. A package name alone cannot tell you whether an experience fits your plans.

Spend where you will feel the difference

Choose a few priorities instead of upgrading everything. If photographs are what you will treasure most, discuss the time needed for them. If sharing the day with loved ones is central, focus on a welcoming setting and a meal. If privacy matters most, start with the venue rather than the decorations.

Keep flexibility where it feels easy: a weekday, a different ceremony hour, a shorter guest list, or one well-chosen floral moment. Ask what each change saves or adds. A good plan gives you clear choices so you can enjoy the day without wondering what you agreed to.
