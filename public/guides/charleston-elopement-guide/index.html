How to elope in Charleston,
beautifully and thoughtfully.

A small wedding deserves just as much care. Here’s how to shape a Lowcountry celebration that feels personal, welcoming, and easy to enjoy.

Local information checked October 11, 2026. Confirm current requirements before booking.

Perhaps it’s just the two of you, a quiet exchange of vows, and dinner somewhere wonderful. Perhaps it’s your closest people gathered in a garden, with photographs and a celebration afterward. Eloping in Charleston can take either shape. The starting point is deciding how you want the day to feel, then choosing the setting and support that make that possible.

ADG Hospitality’s done-for-you elopement experiences are designed for couples and up to 30 guests, subject to the chosen venue’s rules. Our starting investments are Petite at $3,500, Signature at $5,000, and Premier at $7,500. Your written proposal will confirm the location, services, timing, and total investment.

Start with the people and the feeling

Before saving dozens of ceremony photos, settle three things: your approximate guest count, the experience you want, and your overall budget. A ceremony for two with a long portrait session is different from a 30-guest celebration that needs seating, transport, and a private meal. Neither is more meaningful; they simply need different plans.

Choose two or three priorities together. Those might be a private setting, exceptional photographs, beautiful flowers, or the chance to sit down with everyone over dinner. This gives your planner a useful brief and keeps later decisions from turning into a collection of unrelated upgrades.

Choose a date with room around it

Share a preferred date and an alternative if you have one. Ask about weekday availability as well as weekends. Date flexibility gives you more options when a particular ceremony location or professional matters most to you. Do not book nonrefundable travel until the essential reservations are confirmed.

For a destination elopement, think in terms of a short stay rather than a single afternoon. Leave time to arrive, settle in, prepare, and enjoy Charleston afterward. Build your ceremony around the permitted venue window, your photographer’s advice, travel time, and guest comfort; a sunset inspiration photo is not a complete timeline.

Match the location to the guest list

Consider a downtown courtyard, a garden, a private venue, or a coastal ceremony setting. Ask about accessibility, restrooms, seating, parking, privacy, and an indoor alternative. Our Charleston elopement locations guide compares the practical considerations behind these different settings.

A smaller limit at two popular parks

Charleston’s Waterfront Park and White Point Garden allow a maximum of 25 people total at an event, including the couple. That is not a 25-guest allowance. Ask the city how everyone in your proposed event is counted. City park events require approval, and the city asks organizers to contact Recreation at least 21 business days beforehand. See Charleston’s official park permit requirements.

Give the marriage license its own deadline

Charleston County offers an online marriage-license application. South Carolina requires a 24-hour waiting period after the application is filed, and the county says completed applications are processed with the license emailed within two business days. The application fee is $70 plus a transaction fee. Apply with a buffer for weekends, holidays, or corrections. Read the county’s current marriage-license instructions.

The county lists the required identification and supporting documents. Follow its instructions directly; do not send those documents through our inquiry form. Confirm who will perform your ceremony and how the signed license will be returned. Planning help can make the schedule clearer, but you remain responsible for completing your application.

Make the day feel unhurried

A good timeline protects the moments you care about. Include getting ready, travel, vendor arrival, guest welcome, the ceremony, group photographs, portraits, and any meal. Leave breathing room between them. If you are hosting guests, tell them where to be and when, rather than asking them to coordinate several informal messages.

One possible flow is a quiet morning together, afternoon preparation, a late-afternoon ceremony, family photographs, portraits for the two of you, and dinner. This is an illustration rather than a package schedule. Actual timing will depend on your location’s access, the light, travel, and the services in your proposal.

Plan for comfort and changing weather

For any outdoor celebration, identify the backup location before you commit. Ask who makes the weather decision, when it is made, and whether the backup changes your costs or guest capacity. An umbrella is useful for a passing shower, but it does not replace a suitable plan for unsafe conditions.

Small hospitality details matter: water, shade, clear directions, a place for an older guest to sit, and shoes suited to the ground. Mention mobility needs early so your ceremony and portrait locations can be chosen with everyone in mind.

Bring a short brief to your first conversation

Send your date or date range, approximate guest count, preferred setting, overall budget, and your top priorities. You do not need every answer. The goal of the first conversation is to see what is possible and identify the next decisions. A thoughtful proposal should state what is included, what costs extra, who handles each reservation, and what happens if plans change.
